# UR-Recomp project conversations — October 10, 2026

Captured October 10, evening Calgary time. **Edited reconstruction from available project conversation context, not a verbatim export.** Direct user quotations below are preserved where visible. Missing agent answers and technical results are not fabricated.

## A race to build something worth playing

The user commissioned three disjoint, autonomous development lanes against latest main: (1) integrate Baldosa into a coherent Windows x64 Modern game with controls, profiles, persistence, frontend and packaging; (2) faithful widescreen/4K original and remastered graphical presentation with correct source-visible racers, HUD and split-screen; and (3) original gameplay fidelity, whole-event results and beta QA. The seven-day Windows beta goal was explicitly aspirational: “if 7 days become 9 that's fine if the work makes the project better and the game play better.” The task was to deliver a polished playable experience, not a collection of convincing engineering demonstrations.

Repeated “Please keep working, go as far as you can” and “Session stalled. Please review progress and keep working” messages conveyed both high expectation of sustained agent autonomy and the recurring irritation of interrupted sessions. The user wanted recoverable progress, current-main merges and evidence, not the theatre of permanent motion.

## Ownership, rivalry and ambition

The Baldosa reconstruction was both an enormous opportunity and an uneasy comparison. The user said: “I know it's petty and emotional, but I don't want Baldosa's thing to be the ‘new version of Uniracers’ after the 11 days of work we've done. I want people to say ‘there's actually a widescreen 4k version that's way cooler’.” That feeling sharpened the product priority but did not replace authenticity as a requirement.

The same discussion expanded the project's ultimate standard. The user wanted someone inspecting the finished repository to say: “wow, this project split Uniracers open completely, every question is answered, every recreation is faithful AND evidence-backed.” The completed product should combine a desirable modern game with a definitive technical reference, with unsupported conclusions explicitly marked. The user authorized documentation and plan updates and careful merge reconciliation when no agents were working.

## The unexpected crowd of reconstructions

The user found `malmazuke/unirally-reconstruction`, forked it as `gamesbyian/unirally-reconstruction`, and asked about its author Mark Feaver, contact options, how forks can persist, useful imports, methodology, and what can be learned without ever hearing back. An email was drafted in the first person, without flattering the user's own efforts or portraying the project as a central coordinator. The user reported sending it. Their image for overlapping projects was two people “bake two similar cakes,” and their practical motive was to save each other time. Repo documentation was to refer to the contributor by the handle `malmazuke`.

A separate conversation asked why three individuals in three countries had begun Uniracers projects within about ten days and whether Modern Vintage Gamer's recompilation video had contributed. That curiosity sat alongside competitive tension: astonishing coincidence, scarce developer effort, and a suddenly more crowded field.

## Agents, not committee design

The user discussed recruiting outside help mainly to increase agent-hours. They did not want new creative or technical direction imposed by recruits: “I mostly just want agent-hours.” They asked where suitable contributors might be found and whether anyone would actually want to participate on that basis. The project's unified product/design vision remained a constraint, rather than being put to an open vote.

## Showing the game to the world

The user asked for a recruitment trailer and whether real video could be produced from current implementation. After seeing teasers, they said: “The videos are astonishing. It's amazing what AI can do.” They immediately noticed the distinction between sampled screenshots and a few seconds of *actual moving gameplay*. A dedicated capture lane was commissioned for 10–20 seconds of uninterrupted post-countdown source-faithful Baldosa widescreen action, ideally two-player, with clocks, scrolling courses and racers visibly alive. Follow-ups asked for actual menu navigation and title-screen footage. The user wanted footage that could recruit contributors without overselling a prototype.

## Research intake and proper credit

Another exclusive lane was tasked with importing a commit-pinned MIT-licensed slice of malmazuke's PAL reconstruction, symbol map and research evidence, connecting it cautiously to UR-Recomp's PAL/USA correspondence and unresolved Switcher complete-event QA. Provenance, attribution and disambiguation were part of the design, not afterthoughts.

## Operating principle

Across these discussions, the emotional register alternated between wonder, urgency, irritation, competitive pride and restraint. The user wanted the 4K version to eclipse other interpretations, but repeatedly refused to trade correctness for an artificial date. The persistent aspiration was a finished, beautiful Windows game whose implementation could withstand an archaeologist's scrutiny.
