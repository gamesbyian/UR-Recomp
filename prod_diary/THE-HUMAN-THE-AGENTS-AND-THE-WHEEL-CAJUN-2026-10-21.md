## A pretty picture ain't a race

Come October 10, Ian look at de project's early videos an' say, “The videos are astonishing. It's amazing what AI can do.”

Cher, dat sentence got more life in it than fifteen pages o' sprint bookkeeping. Dey had shown him somethin' what astonished him, an' de record let me say so without pretendin' I peeked inside his head. But de next request tell as much o' de tale as de first. He want actual moving gameplay, a whole little stretch of de wheel doing what a wheel supposed to do. Still pictures an' good editing could make a wonderful promise; a real run had to collect on it.

Dis director got a habit what make de story interesting. He see a little miracle. He give it credit. Den he point to de part what ain't there yet. No need to invent a big argument around dat exchange. De contrast already got rhythm.

De agents eventually gather native footage an' examine gaps in animated pose coverage. Dey had to distinguish a convincing preview from actual post-countdown play. All dat source-bound fuss come back to one perfectly human desire: show de racers alive.

## De smallest hero bring de biggest toolbox

Go back to September 28. Ian ask whether *Uniracers*, dat Super Nintendo oddity with no rider on its unicycle, be worth remaking. Maybe somebody think dat a small job. Draw a wheel, lay down a track, teach it to flip, bring out a modern title screen, go home for gumbo.

But Ian keep pressin' toward a remake what deserved de original name. De agents start opening ROM structures, SRAM, compression, racer state, camera rules an' timing. A two-megabyte USA ROM an' eight KiB o' SRAM don't sound like a whole epic, but dem bytes guard de difference between a trick what earns points an' one what don't. If de wheel land wrong, de code can't argue de picture was pretty.

I read de old account an' find one good comic contrast: an army of agents with tracing tools an' source comparison rigs spendin' weeks on de exact habits of a fictional riderless wheel. Funny, yes. Also a respectable answer to a serious design demand. Ian want a modern game folk can enjoy without sneaking in new physics under old colors.

The project need two things what don't always like each other: a game dat feel alive, an' scientific proof dat it stay true. Dat friction supplies enough plot for several volumes.

## “Keep going” become de camp bell

Ian give de agents room to act. He tell 'em keep working, recover from stalled sessions, an' find useful tasks when CI get stuck. Dat sound simple till de automation start eating its own tail. De October 5 account report **108 active or queued Actions runs** coming from a handful o' agents. De machines invent a parade of checks, then get trapped waiting behind de parade.

I'd be tempted to write Ian stomped around de office with steam coming out his ears. I won't. De archived requests don't prove dat picture, an' a chronicler ought to know when a cartoon is only a cartoon. What de words *do* establish is how much initiative he wanted: don't just stand at de runner's door staring; find somethin' else worthwhile, preserve your work, come back with actual evidence.

Dis where de agent collaborator become a real literary character without pretendin' it a human soul. Some agents produce clever science. Some produce ingenious code. Some leave so many branches behind de next agent need a search party. All can be capable in one moment an' confidently wrong in another. De continuity come from GitHub history, explicit records, journals an' Ian's direction, not from one magical mind that secretly inhabit every session.

He might call de agent a friend in de book's telling. Friendship here is de warm working relationship visible in questions, jokes, challenges, corrections an' trust. It ain't proof of a private emotional life behind every green checkmark.

## De other builders arrive upriver

October 10 bring Baldosa's work an' malmazuke's reconstruction into de picture. New resources! New techniques! Maybe even a better route to de native Windows game. Also one mighty human moment.

Ian say: “I know it's petty and emotional, but I don't want Baldosa's thing to be the ‘new version of Uniracers’ after the 11 days of work we've done.”

No need for de narrator to hunt for a secret feeling; de director hand one over in plain language. He want a 4K widescreen version what players reckon cooler, an' he want de repository so well researched future folks can inspect the old game without another hundred unanswered mysteries.

An ordinary rivalry story might turn every outside developer into a bad guy. Dis one got a smarter twist. Good work from elsewhere could improve Ian's own game. So de agents study it, pin de source, check whether PAL findings apply to de USA target, an' keep de original game's mechanical truth in charge.

Dat mix of competitive pride and willingness to borrow tested knowledge give de project more depth than a cartoon race for bragging rights. Ian wasn't choosing between beauty and evidence, or between ambition and cooperation. He was askin' de agents to carry all of 'em without dropping de wheel.

## De wheel still gotta look like de wheel

A computer can count poses, index sprites an' produce very detailed 4× artwork. But de computer's chart ain't gonna tell Ian whether de unicycle looks like *Uniracers*. He saw candidate procedural racer forms an' withheld approval of de likeness. Dat veto was a real creative event, even if de engineer could show accurate source mapping and technical coverage.

Same trouble visit de menus. Ian want widescreen, proper controller flow, Modern destinations, useful records an' settings, but no soulless grey control panel wearing de old game's name tag. De color, de movement, de little wheel's personality, an' de way a real native title hands off to actual menus: all dat is part of de game a person meets.

An independent test can tell you a button is reachable. It cannot decide whether Ian recognizes de childhood game's strange little hero in a freshly drawn character. Here de director's eyes remain part of de final authority.

## Zero ain't an insult, just an honest number

At de October 20 cutoff de release accounting still showed **0/45** independently accepted complete USA original/native events an' **0/12** Windows release gates. Sounds like nobody did a lick o' work, if you don't know what de numbers measure. But plenty real things *had* worked in bounded tests. Dat denominator had very sharp teeth.

Take de NMI/RTI mystery. Saved PC bytes differ. Does dat mean de next opcode came from de wrong address? Only if you can show actual execution consumed dat address. Another agent needed to distinguish stack observation from compiled RTI and host-scheduler behavior. An attractive theory ain't earned a race trophy just because it arrive wrapped in binary data.

Dis is where Ian's demands and de agents' skeptical work make a peculiar good partnership. He want the beautiful finished thing. De agents bring hypotheses an' sometimes irresistible explanations. Independent QA say whether de exact proposed result passed. An honest 'not yet' ought to protect de dream better than a dishonest 'done'.

## A memory for agents what keep losin' de room number

By October 20 de team face a new mystery: how do you keep ten changing assignments from turning into ten lost histories? Ian suggest durable journals. Agent lanes, task IDs, source branches, a GitHub-first process an' a Drive recovery Vault come into de plot. Now dem agents excavate dey own recent labor with almost de same caution they use on de old ROM.

One unpublished ZIP might be precious, another dangerous. A PR can prove code was written without proving de feature works. A journal can say where an agent stopped without giving its next worker permission to believe everything dat agent suspected. More memory without better source authority just make a bigger swamp.

And dat bring me back to de man an' his agent friend. Ian want a book where he shows up as a particular person, not a request dispenser, an' where de agents show wit, fallibility, curiosity an' practical companionship rather than only testing machinery. His actual words on October 21 ask for “a fascinating and fun story of a particular human and his agent friend remaking a game.”

I can honor dat with scenes, direct quotes, competing ideas and good endings when evidence earns 'em. I can't honor it by inventing tears, anger, silent pride, secret conversations or a single immortal agent who personally remembers every job.

De wheel still got no rider. De story got a director, a crowd o' software helpers, an' a very long track still unwinding ahead. No fake winner's ribbon at dis point, cher. De race itself is interesting enough.
