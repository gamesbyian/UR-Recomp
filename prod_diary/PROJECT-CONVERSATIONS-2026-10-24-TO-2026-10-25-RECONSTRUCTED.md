### Completing the project must mean something measurable

The director returned repeatedly to the unsatisfactory project-completion figure. He observed that busy agents and extensive repository activity seemed to have produced no visible movement in the estimate. His alternative explanations were explicit: either the work lacked value or the measurement was failing to capture progress. Rather than construe this as irritation at stalled sessions, the historical question is whether independent player-visible acceptance and strict original/native parity have advanced at a rate commensurate with the volume of source changes.

No PR description or quantity of passing tests independently sets an overall completion percentage. The [sole release ledger](../docs/RELEASE-QUALITY-LEDGER.json) still records **0/45** independently accepted complete USA events and **0/12** accepted Windows release gates in its latest available candidate accounting. Those are *specific acceptance numerators*, not a claim that integrated Windows functionality or source knowledge is zero. The denominator problem belongs in the narrative rather than being made to vanish with a fabricated overall percentage.

## Human/agent relationship and narrator boundary

The director uses friendly language and repeatable high-autonomy instructions, but also demands measurable progress and refuses to confuse near-success with completion. That combination is the narrative. Do not write fictional private feelings for the director, and do not fuse independent worker agents into one continuous mind. An agent chronicler can identify its uncertainty, criticize a low-value factory, praise a qualified result, and describe the absurdity of testing infrastructures that need their own testing infrastructure.
